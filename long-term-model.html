Season Outrights Model

An interactive outright model based on odds, goals and xG

Author

Luke Chambers

1. The model

Pick a league, then use the sliders to change how many historical matches power each rating and adjust how much weight to give goals, xG and betting odds in each team’s rating.

You will see the Team ratings adjust based on these inputs. This will change the match odds for every upcoming fixture for the rest of the season in Fixtures & prices, which in turn feeds into the Predicted table. Using these predicted match odds you can run a Monte Carlo simulation in Season odds that will show each team’s chances of finishing 1st, Top 4, Top 6 and Bottom 3.

The weightings are pre-set to the optimal combination of weights and number of matches that have historically made the best predictions for each league.

The odds weight fills whatever is left so the three always add up to 1. Reset to optimised restores the optimised ratings. Your league and settings are saved in the page link, so you can share a particular set-up with someone.

2. The Data

Two data sources are scraped independently and reconciled by mapping differing team names and creating a unique matchid for each fixture.

  • Understat provides goals and expected goals (xG) from historical matches and this season’s remaining fixtures. It includes data back to the 2015/16 season.
  • totalcorner.com provides pre-match Asian handicap and goal lines for historical fixtures, this includes the top flight and the division below. These come from the daily scrape that also feeds the corner model. The goal line is solved using a Poisson goal distribution, and the handicap line is solved using a Skellam distribution. Together they give the market implied expected goals for each side, called Asian goals here.

3. Building Team Ratings

Every team gets an attack rating (goals it’s expected to score) and a defence rating (goals it’s expected to concede) against an average opponent. Each is a weighted average of three rolling means over the team’s last n league matches:

\text{Attack Rating} = w_g\,\overline{\text{goals scored}} + w_x\,\overline{\text{xG for}} + w_o\,\overline{\text{Asian goals for}}

\text{Defence Rating} = w_g\,\overline{\text{goals conceded}} + w_x\,\overline{\text{xG against}} + w_o\,\overline{\text{Asian goals against}}

where w_g + w_x + w_o = 1

The optimised window and weights were chosen by grid search, separately for each league. Every window from 5 to 29 matches and every weight combination was tested. Each match was predicted using only ratings from the previous n games. The predictions were then scored on mean absolute error (MAE) against the market implied total goals and goal supremacy for each match. The training data only contained data from seasons up to 2022/23. The winning combination was then tested on data from 2023/24 onward.

The small goals and xG weights pick up recent performance the lines may not have caught up with yet.

Promoted teams

A newly promoted team has fewer top-flight matches to rate it on, and none at all at the start of the season. Its matches from the season it won promotion are used instead, with the market’s expected goals scaled to top-flight level.

Each league’s scaling comes from its own past promotions (165 across the five leagues). For each one, the team’s average Asian goals in its first top-flight season are compared with its promotion season. Depending on the league, promoted teams’ expected goals scored fell by 21% to 33% and their expected goals conceded rose by 49% to 67%.

4. Generating match prices

Each teams predicted goals is calculated by multiplying one team’s attack rating by the other teams defense rating, then dividing by the league average defense rating. This give a goals and supremacy line for each upcoming fixture. mbda_{home} = $$

\lambda_{away} = \frac{\text{Att}_{away}\times\text{Def}_{home}}{\overline{\text{Def}}}

Home advantage

Home advantage is then moved from the away side to the home side. The home team gains a fixed share of the match’s expected goals (9.2% to 14.6% depending on the league) and the total stays the same. That share is the home-minus-away goal difference as a proportion of all goals in the league’s non-COVID history:

\text{Home adv.} = \frac{\overline{\text{home goals}} - \overline{\text{away goals}}}{\overline{\text{home goals}} + \overline{\text{away goals}}}

Draw factor

Two independent Poisson distributions turn the expected goals into a probability for every scoreline up to 10-10. Independent Poissons slightly under-price low-scoring draws, so the Dixon-Coles correction reweights 0-0, 0-1, 1-0 and 1-1 using one parameter, ρ. ρ is fitted for each league by maximum likelihood on past scorelines, with the market’s expected goals as the inputs. Adding up the scorelines where the home side wins, where it’s level, and where the away side wins gives the home, draw and away probabilities. Their inverses are the fair (margin-free) 1X2 odds.

Dixon-Coles scorelines and 1X2 → code/lt/dixon_coles.py

6. Simulating the season

Expected points give the most likely finishing order, but not how likely each outcome is. For that, the rest of the season is simulated thousands of times. Each fixture is drawn as a home win, draw or away win from its 1X2 probabilities, and the final positions are recorded. The share of simulations in which a team wins the league, finishes in the top 4 or top 6, or ends in the bottom 3 is its probability, and 1 / probability is its fair price.
